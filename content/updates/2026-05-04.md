Three patch releases shipped this week, each closing a specific gap surfaced through internal review. The pattern is rapid pre-release iteration: the spec is being hardened in place rather than accumulating issues for a single milestone, which is what pre-1.0 status is for.

## v0.1.2: Disavowal and supersedes scope, plus consumer-side JWKS handling

The v0.1.2 release on April 30 was a security patch. Two of the spec's claim types, `disavowal` (publisher disclaims content not theirs) and `supersedes` (publisher marks content as replaced), were originally scoped broadly: a publisher could disavow or supersede arbitrary URLs. That breadth was a problem. A bad actor controlling `example.com` could publish disavowals against `competitor.com`'s pages, and a credulous consumer might apply them.

v0.1.2 constrains both claim types. `disavowal` is now limited to publisher self-statements (taking back something the publisher previously said) and impersonation defense (renouncing a forgery falsely attributed to the publisher). `supersedes` is similarly constrained to URLs and documents the publisher controls or formerly controlled. Third-party-targeting variants of either claim are now out of conformance at Standard and Strict tiers. Documents valid under v0.1.1 that already used these claims correctly remain valid under v0.1.2; documents that contained out-of-scope third-party variants now fail conformance.

The same release added section §4.7, specifying consumer-side JWKS handling: caching cap of 24 hours matching `valid_until`, key change detection on unrecognized `kid`, and the differential-fetch policy when sudden full key replacement is observed. JWKS is the JSON Web Key Set, the publisher's set of public keys; getting consumer behavior right when a publisher rotates keys is what allows the protocol to survive long-running deployments.

Full details in the [v0.1.2 changelog entry](/spec/changelog/#012---2026-04-30).

## v0.1.3: S4 (URL ownership) semantic alignment

The same day as v0.1.2, v0.1.3 fixed a rule misalignment in the Standard-tier conformance set. The original S4 rule (URL ownership) flagged `personnel.spokespeople[].verification` URLs as ownership violations, but those URLs are third-party identity attestation by design (a spokesperson points to their GitHub profile, for example, to prove they are who they say they are). The §3.5 example explicitly showed `https://github.com/thegigachav` as the canonical pattern, but the S4 rule contradicted that example.

v0.1.3 corrects S4 to enumerate third-party-allowed fields explicitly: `pointer.url`, `disavowal.disavowed[].url`, `official_channels.community[].url`, and `personnel.spokespeople[].verification`. Other fields with URLs (canonical URLs, supersedes targets) continue to require ownership. No artifact change is required: the live `llmo.json` document was correct as-published; the rule needed to catch up to the example.

Full details in the [v0.1.3 changelog entry](/spec/changelog/#013---2026-04-30).

## v0.1.4: Schema completeness pass

On May 1, v0.1.4 closed a different category of drift: prose-vs-schema. The specification text is the source of truth, but it is also long, and details specified in prose can fail to make it into the JSON Schema that validators run. v0.1.4 audited the schema against the prose and tightened two fields: `identity.founded` now requires year, year-month, or full date format (no more free-text "yesterday"), and the `claim.type` field now uses a `oneOf` enforcing the bipartite "core type or namespaced extension" rule that §3.5 and §3.6 describe.

Documents that previously passed schema validation but contained malformed types or non-date `founded` values will now be rejected by conforming validators. No artifact change is required for the project's live `llmo.json`; the document was correct as-published.

Full details in the [v0.1.4 changelog entry](/spec/changelog/#014---2026-05-01).

## What is happening next

Three patches in five days reflects pre-release status, not panic. Each patch closes a specific gap and tightens conformance. The expected next release, v0.1.5, will introduce per-claim signature verification (rule X6) and complete the algorithm matrix in tooling.
