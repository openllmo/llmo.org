# The Three O's

LLMO comprises three concurrent layers. Each governs a distinct domain. Together they form the complete architecture.

## Optimization

The external representation layer. Ensures that an entity is represented accurately across LLM-driven systems.

Scope:

- Visibility in LLM outputs
- Citation presence and answer inclusion
- Factual consistency and entity resolution
- Model preference and semantic authority
- Reputation resilience
- External truth alignment

This layer overlaps with AEO and GEO but treats representation as an engineering problem rather than a content problem.

## Operations

The internal truth governance layer. Makes organizational truth machine-usable and governable.

Scope:

- Internal knowledge integrity
- Workflow instrumentation
- Policy-bounded decision support
- Retrieval and grounding
- Execution control, logging, and evaluation
- Governance and auditability

An entity cannot maintain accurate external representation without internal truth discipline.

## Orchestration

The deterministic coordination layer. Governs how models, tools, humans, memory, and policies interact in production workflows.

Scope:

- Agent flow control and task routing
- Memory coordination and state management
- Replayability
- Policy enforcement and human checkpoints
- Harness design

Orchestration is what makes LLMO executable. Without it, Optimization is positioning and Operations is bookkeeping.

---

## Relationship

Optimization governs external perception. Operations governs internal precision. Orchestration governs execution.

The three layers are not phases. They are concurrent. An entity that optimizes its external surface without operational truth integrity is building on an unsound foundation. An entity with internal truth but no orchestration layer cannot execute at machine speed.

The complete system requires all three.
