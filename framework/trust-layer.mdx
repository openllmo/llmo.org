# Trust Layer

LLMO extends from optimization into trust infrastructure. This section specifies the trust primitives and their requirements.

## The insufficiency of visibility

The current information environment is degraded by content farming, fake reviews, salted directories, inconsistent entity information, unverifiable claims, model hallucination from unreliable sources, and probabilistic compression that blends high and low quality material.

<Warning>
A model that surfaces an entity but associates it with stale, conflicting, or fabricated claims produces worse outcomes than omission.
</Warning>

Visibility is necessary but insufficient. The system must also determine: what is true, who asserted it, when it was true, what superseded it, whether it can be verified, and whether a machine can trust it enough to act.

## Trust primitives

Optimization governs whether models surface an entity. Trust governs whether models should believe what they surface.

| Primitive | Function |
|---|---|
| **Provenance** | Traces the chain from source to claim to representation |
| **Signed truth** | Binds a claim to an accountable origin with cryptographic or attestation backing |
| **Verification** | Confirms a claim against authoritative sources |
| **Freshness** | Determines whether a claim is within its validity window |
| **Supersession** | Tracks which claims have been replaced by newer authoritative statements |
| **Audit** | Provides a replayable record of claim production, verification, and use |
| **Replay** | Re-executes decision pathways to confirm or debug prior outputs |

These are structural requirements, not optional extensions.

## Economic basis

When models inform or execute decisions, the cost of bad information compounds. Stale compliance claims can trigger regulatory exposure. Hallucinated capabilities cited in procurement can break contracts.

Trust infrastructure is an economic requirement for environments where machine outputs have downstream consequences.

## Perception observability

The trust layer includes the ability to observe and measure machine perception over time:

- How an entity appears across models
- Where it is cited, omitted, or distorted
- Which claims are unstable
- How representation changes over time
- Where the source graph weakened or strengthened
- What interventions improved representation

This constitutes a diff engine for semantic reputation and truth integrity.

## The trust artifact

Content is an insufficient substrate for machine trust. The protocol requires a structured, machine-readable, verifiable artifact that expresses:

- Entity identity
- Canonical claims and their truth status
- Verification timestamps and freshness windows
- Supersession chains
- Supporting sources and their trust tiers
- Applicable confidence or verification levels

This artifact is the **truth pack**. Its reference implementation is [`llmo.json`](/protocol/llmo-json).
